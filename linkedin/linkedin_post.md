📊 New portfolio project: **Customer Segmentation & Retention Analytics | Financial Services**

Retail banks often treat their whole customer base the same way, even though value and engagement can differ wildly within it. I built an end-to-end case study on a **fully synthetic** book of 4,000 fictional banking customers to explore three questions:

🔹 Who creates the value? Premier Advocates are 14% of customers but 42% of estimated revenue.
🔹 Who is quietly drifting? "Affluent Drifters" (high value, low engagement) are 4.6% of customers, yet they carry 34% of the expected revenue at risk.
🔹 Which early warning signs matter? An unused banking app showed 3.0× the average attrition rate. One "obvious" signal (high card utilisation) added nothing.

What's inside:
✅ Reproducible Python pipeline: synthetic data generation, logged cleaning with no imputation, feature engineering
✅ A two-axis segmentation (revenue proxy × engagement index), stress-tested against alternative weights and k-means
✅ A logistic-regression attrition model (AUC 0.81 on held-out data, with honest caveats)
✅ Hypothetical interventions sized on explicit assumptions, plus a holdout pilot design
✅ An interactive HTML dashboard, Excel workings with live formulas, and an 8-slide case study
✅ 39 automated validation checks and unit tests

Transparency note: I scoped the problem, set the requirements and reviewed the results. The code, synthetic data and materials were generated with an AI assistant (Claude) under my direction. All data and findings are synthetic and illustrative.
